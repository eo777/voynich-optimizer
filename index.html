Echo · Frequency and information

Voynich optimizer

Can proposed spelling transformations preserve information while matching how words are used across manuscript sections?

Each starting spelling receives a distinct value, weighted by its observed frequency. A spelling change can preserve that value; a merge loses distinctions when the output no longer identifies which starting value reached it. The optimizer balances that ambiguity against disagreement with observed word frequencies.

This page explores saved fits from the simple Echo joint optimizer. Selecting a word replays its fitted routes. The values are synthetic word identities, not decoded meanings; the routes are candidate systems, not established historical ancestry. The proposed section ordering and permitted transformations are assumptions of the experiment.
