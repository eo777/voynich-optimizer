ECHO 7 · HISTORICAL REFERENCE · 15 OCTOBER 2026

Abbreviation can preserve nearly all word identity while word frequencies still change substantially.

These two measurements answer different questions. Collisions ask whether the written form still identifies its expanded word. Frequency mismatch asks whether a fixed starting distribution predicts later usage. A text can score well on one and poorly on the other.

This is a measured reference, not a medieval universal. The abbreviation table uses real editor-aligned manuscript tokens. The passage table uses a separate, unabbreviated Clementine Vulgate edition. Neither is a historical sequence reconstructed from our Voynich model.

1. Real medieval abbreviation pairs

Each expanded spelling is a distinct value W; its attested manuscript form is Y. We count the actual aligned pairs, then measure 1 − H(W | Y) / H(W). The decoder sees only the isolated written form, without surrounding words. Multiple spellings of one expanded word do not themselves lose information; several expanded words sharing one written form can.

What an actual collision looks like

Fontenay mͦ appears with editor expansions modo 9 times, monachus 5, monacho 1, and monachi 1. For these 16 tokens, the form alone leaves 1.491 bits of uncertainty; the best single-form choice makes 7 errors (43.75%). This is a real local ambiguity, but it occupies only 16 of 19,551 tokens.
Vulgate Psalm samples ge. expands to generationem 4 times, generatione once, and gemitum once across the pooled witnesses. Some other counted collisions are spelling variants such as quacumque/quacunque, so word-spelling loss must not be equated with loss of meaning.

Saint-Victor illustrates the distinction between “ambiguous” and “lost”: 12.918% of tokens use a spelling that has more than one observed expansion, but a context-free majority decoder makes only 0.894% errors. A frequent form can be overwhelmingly associated with one expansion. The release also includes normalization and occasional surprising pairs, such as ĩ → corpus; the table counts those literally and does not claim every collision was a deliberate scribal abbreviation.

Pooling different witnesses can create collisions between different scribal conventions. “Witness known” uses H(W|Y,D) relative to H(W|D), where D identifies the manuscript or charter. This does not supply sentence context. Most individual samples are short, which limits the chance of observing every possible expansion. The per-witness and period records are retained in the metrics file; the 19 strictly fifteenth-century Psalm witnesses contribute 6,147 lexical tokens, with 99.976% pooled word information retained and no observed common-to-common collision.

The exact forward frequency mismatch is zero to numerical precision. With the actual aligned channel P(Y|W), multiplying the expanded-word frequencies by that channel reproduces the observed abbreviation frequencies: maximum TV across these checks is 1.6e-17. This is an algebraic consequence of measuring the same tokens. Collisions do not force forward frequency mismatch. The last column instead asks what happens after a decoder always selects one expansion per surface; this can distort expanded-word frequencies.

2. Frequency drift with no spelling change at all

As a separate control, the full 73-book Clementine Vulgate supplies 612,195 ordinary letter-word tokens. We divide its canonical order into 20 disjoint chains of eight passages with exactly the Echo section sizes: 2411, 1798, 3107, 2212, 1648, 7412, 4598, 6812. Each chain has 29,998 tokens. The mapping is always identity and retains 100% of every initial word identity. The initial word frequencies are held fixed, just as in the Echo diagnostic, and compared with each later passage.

All values are averages over 20 natural chains or 60 shuffled chains from three fixed seeds. Common words are selected once from the initial passage (count ≥10). “Common + other” retains each common word and pools every remaining word into one bucket. “Within common” renormalizes each passage within that frozen cohort, so it does not measure their total prevalence. These are distinct denominators, not interchangeable scores.

The natural chain means range from 51.32% to 65.39%; final mismatch ranges from 48.84% to 68.62%. The shuffled control removes topic and sequence structure while preserving the full vocabulary frequency distribution, showing how much mismatch can come from finite samples of a large inflected vocabulary. The large all-word TV is therefore not automatically evidence of information loss.

This does not set a 59% acceptable threshold for Voynich. This Latin corpus has 46,476 word types, including 21,718 hapax types. Vocabulary size, morphology, genre, and repetition have not been matched to Voynich. Some chains cross biblical book boundaries; all boundaries are inventoried. The experiment demonstrates the distinction between fixed-usage mismatch and identity preservation, rather than explaining a particular Voynich transformation. The 1598 recension is also not a fifteenth-century scribal witness.

3. Published context and the limits of this reference

Camps, Vidal-Gorène and Vernet describe medieval abbreviations as a many-to-many relation and report 97.02% expansion accuracy for their augmented contextual model, including 97.92% on ambiguous inputs (Table 4). Those are supervised expansion accuracies, not entropy-retention percentages. Their complete image/segmentation/expansion pipeline has 31.81% word error in its best reported configuration (Table 5), which includes recognition and segmentation failures. Context can recover information unavailable from an isolated form. Read the primary paper.

  • Our information labels are expanded spellings, not inferred meanings. Spelling variants and editorial corrections can raise apparent loss.
  • Empirical collisions are only those seen in the retained samples. Rare forms observed once are not evidence that their abbreviation was universally unambiguous. No population confidence interval or universal medieval bound is claimed.
  • Signs and combining marks are preserved, lowercased and NFC-normalized. Flattened source layers can omit graphical distinctions. A sensitivity excludes unclear expanded targets that still contain abbreviation-like or private-use signs; the result remains almost unchanged: Vulgate Psalm 101: 48 manuscript witnesses: 14 excluded, 99.940% retained; Dated Latin manuscripts: 101 samples: 125 excluded, 99.899% retained; Fontenay: 104 charters: 260 excluded, 99.881% retained; Saint-Victor Latin 14525: one hand: 0 excluded, 99.710% retained.
  • The Vulgate Psalm witnesses are copies of related text, not 48 independent compositions, and are not ordered as stages of a language evolving.
  • Actual paired transformations use the known editorial correspondence. Echo must infer a correspondence from unrelated sections. The reference is an achievable form-preservation example, not an equally difficult fitting task.

Reproduction and sources

Run historical_reference.py, historical_reference_vulgate_usage.py, then historical_reference_report.py from this study directory or by full path. No source is downloaded and no model is trained. All pair-stream SHA-256 hashes are checked against the retained catalogue; the Vulgate archive is checked against its acquisition lock. Entropy is independently checked as H(W,Y)−H(Y), witness conditioning by adding the witness to the observed form, and TV as one minus distributional overlap. Synthetic one-to-one and many-to-one channels verify the direction of the test.

258 corpus/witness entropy checks; maximum formula discrepancy 3.7e-14 bits. The output contains metrics and short examples, not redistributed corpus text or model arrays.
